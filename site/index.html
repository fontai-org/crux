Install, step by step

Didn't work? Do it like this.

Claude Code

  1. Open a terminal and run:
    claude plugin install crux --marketplace fontai/crux
  2. Error unknown option '--marketplace'? Your Claude Code is older. Run these two lines instead, or update Claude Code:
    claude plugin marketplace add fontai/crux claude plugin install crux@fontai
  3. Start a new Claude Code session. Already running sessions won't see CRUX.
  4. Try it: crux path/to/document.pdf. The first run may ask whether CRUX may run. Choose Yes, and don't ask again.
  5. Check that it's installed: claude plugin list must show crux@fontai.

Claude app web · desktop · phone

  5. Upload crux.zip as a new skill and make sure it's switched on.

What you get

One screen instead of forty pages.

Built for people who get long, often AI-written analyses and have to make a decision based on them.

01

Verdict first

Approve, Revise, Return or Missing info, with one sentence why. Many readers stop here.

02

The summary

Main message, key points, risks and gaps, and questions worth asking the author. Each point links to its page or section.

03

A quality score

Seven criteria on a 1–5 scale, each backed by evidence. Key claims are checked on the web, and cited sources are checked to see whether they exist.

04

The brief behind it

What was the author probably asked to do, and why? It helps when you don't know where the document came from.

How it works

Say “crux” and hand it the document.

Markdown, Word, PDF or pasted text. CRUX replies in your language, whatever the document's language.

1

Give it the document

crux report.pdf, or attach the file and write “crux”.

2

Answer four questions

What to do, how deep, the author's brief (if you have it), and what you need to decide.

3

It reads and checks

In Claude Code, separate helpers do the research and return only short findings, so your session stays light.

4

You decide

The answer stays in the chat. Ask if you want it saved or exported to PDF.

How deep

You always know how long it takes.

Pick the depth before it starts. Long documents (50+ pages) take about half as long again.

Quick~1–3 min

Only the document itself: consistency, gaps, typical AI defects. No web.

Standard~5–10 min · recommended

Plus a web check of the claims the conclusions rest on, and of the sources it cites.

Deep~15–30 min

Plus independent research by parallel helpers, and every source checked.

Example answer

What it looks like.

A fictional 40-page analysis, the one from the video: should a 120-person company move to a four-day week? CRUX's real answer, shortened.

VERDICT: Return. The productivity study the whole business case rests on could not be found anywhere, and the document contradicts its own numbers. A pilot can still make sense, but approve it on its own terms. Brief: not supplied, guessed (high confidence, §1.1) Your decision: whether to approve a 3-month pilot SUMMARY Main message: 100 % pay for 80 % of the time, staggered days off; a 6-month pilot with 56 people, then company-wide (§15.2). • Financial model: net EUR 412k a year, 7-month payback (§8.5) • It depends on a +27 % productivity gain "from 340 logistics firms" (§5.4) RECOMMENDATION — for your decision • Approve only the pilot, not the rollout or hiring 6 people (§15.2). • Set pass/fail criteria without the 27 %: output ≥ 95 % of baseline (§10.4). RISKS AND GAPS ⚠ The study behind the 27 % looks made up: its site doesn't exist (§5.4) ⚠ Net benefit is EUR 1.2M in some places, EUR 412k in others (§8.5) ⚠ The warehouse has 46 people in one place and 64 in another (§4.2, §7.9) ⚠ The pilot's own targets don't fit: +15 % per hour × 80 % of hours = 92 %, below the 95 % pass mark (§14.2 vs §10.4) ⚠ The Risks section lists no risks (§13) QUESTIONS FOR THE AUTHOR 1. Can you share the study? If not, what is the case without the 27 %? 2. Which is right: EUR 412k or 1.2M, 46 or 64 staff? 3. What does the pilot alone cost, and how do we roll it back? SCORING (Standard · brief guessed) Overall 2.2/5 → Return Accuracy 2 · Support 2 · Specificity 3 · Consistency 1 Usefulness 3 · Depth 2 · Alignment 4 (approximate)
What CRUX promises

Strict, and never makes things up.

No made-up sources

It cites only pages it actually opened. Everything else is marked unverified.

Strict scoring

AI tends to go easy on AI-written text. CRUX needs the same evidence for praise as for criticism.

Your data stays yours

Web searches use generic terms about the topic, never names or numbers from your document.

Always up to date

It checks once a day and tells you when a new version is out.
