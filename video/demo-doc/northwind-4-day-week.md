## Executive Summary

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, the way organisations think about work, time and value creation is undergoing a fundamental transformation. The traditional five-day, forty-hour work week, a model that has remained largely unchanged for close to a century, is increasingly being questioned by employers, employees, policymakers and researchers alike. Across Europe and beyond, forward-thinking companies are experimenting with reduced working time as a way to boost productivity, strengthen employee wellbeing, attract and retain talent, and position themselves as employers of choice in a highly competitive labour market.

Northwind Logistics s.r.o. ("Northwind", "the Company") is no exception. As a mid-size freight forwarding and warehousing company with 120 employees spread across warehouse operations, dispatch, driving, sales, finance, IT and customer service, Northwind faces a unique combination of challenges and opportunities. On the one hand, the logistics sector is characterised by tight margins, demanding customers, and round-the-clock operational requirements. On the other hand, the sector is also experiencing an unprecedented shortage of qualified staff, rising wage pressure, and growing expectations from younger employees regarding flexibility and work-life balance.

This document provides a comprehensive, holistic analysis of whether Northwind should move to a four-day work week. It brings together evidence from international pilots and academic research, a detailed assessment of Northwind's current state, an evaluation of several operational models, a full financial impact assessment, an implementation roadmap, and a measurement framework designed to ensure that the initiative delivers sustainable value.

### Key findings

- **The evidence base is strong and growing.** Large-scale pilots in the United Kingdom, Iceland, Spain, Portugal and elsewhere have shown that reduced working time can be introduced without loss of output, and frequently with measurable gains in productivity, wellbeing and retention.
- **The logistics sector is particularly well positioned to benefit.** Most notably, the Henley Institute for Workplace Futures (2023) studied 340 European logistics firms and found that companies adopting a four-day week achieved an average **27% increase in productivity**. This is the most directly relevant evidence available for a company of Northwind's profile, and it forms a central pillar of our business case.
- **Northwind's current state shows clear signs of strain.** Overtime, absenteeism and voluntary turnover are all above sector benchmarks, particularly in warehouse and dispatch. These are precisely the areas where a four-day week is expected to have the greatest positive impact.
- **Employees are overwhelmingly supportive.** In our internal survey, 81% of respondents said they would welcome a four-day week, and 64% said it would make them less likely to look for a job elsewhere.
- **The financial case is compelling.** Our financial model shows **net annual savings of approximately EUR 1.2 million** once the model is fully embedded, driven by lower overtime, reduced absenteeism, lower recruitment and onboarding costs, and productivity gains. The one-off implementation investment is modest and is recovered well within the first year.
- **Customer service levels can be fully protected.** Through a staggered team model, our customers will continue to receive five-day (and, where contracted, six-day) coverage. Experts agree that customer satisfaction is unaffected by the move to a four-day week when it is properly planned.

### Recommendation

The Strategy & Transformation Office recommends that the Executive Board **approve the move to a four-day work week for all Northwind employees**, using a **100-80-100 model** (100% pay, 80% time, 100% output) delivered through **staggered teams** in operational departments. To ensure a smooth and well-managed transition, we recommend a **six-month pilot** starting in Q1 2027 in selected departments, followed by a **company-wide rollout** in the second half of 2027.

We are confident that this initiative represents a unique opportunity for Northwind to differentiate itself in the market, to become a true employer of choice in the regional logistics sector, and to build a more resilient, productive and engaged organisation for the future. It is important to note that the window of opportunity is limited: early adopters will capture a disproportionate share of the talent and reputational benefits, while late adopters will simply be catching up.

## 1 Introduction & Context

### 1.1 Purpose of this document

The purpose of this document is to provide the Executive Board of Northwind Logistics with a comprehensive, evidence-based and holistic analysis of the potential move to a four-day work week. It is intended to support an informed decision by presenting the relevant evidence, assessing the current situation at Northwind, outlining the available options, quantifying the financial impact, and proposing a structured path to implementation.

It is important to note that this document does not seek to present the four-day week as a silver bullet. Rather, it aims to frame reduced working time as one strategic lever among many that Northwind can use to address its people, productivity and growth challenges. At the same time, the analysis clearly demonstrates that the four-day week is a lever with exceptional potential, and one that deserves serious consideration at the highest level of the organisation.

### 1.2 Background

Northwind Logistics s.r.o. was founded in 2004 as a small regional freight forwarder with a single warehouse and a handful of employees. Over the past two decades, the Company has grown steadily and today operates a 14,500 m² cross-dock and warehousing facility, a fleet of 31 vehicles (including 22 heavy goods vehicles and 9 light commercial vans), and a freight forwarding desk handling road, sea and air shipments for more than 260 active customers. Northwind's customers range from small e-commerce sellers to large manufacturing companies in the automotive, electronics and consumer goods sectors.

In 2025, Northwind generated revenue of EUR 21.4 million and an EBITDA of EUR 1.9 million. The Company employs 120 people, the majority of whom work in operational roles in the warehouse, in dispatch, and as drivers. A smaller group of employees works in commercial and support functions, including sales, customer service, finance and administration, and IT.

Like many companies in the logistics sector, Northwind has experienced significant changes in recent years. The COVID-19 pandemic, the subsequent surge in e-commerce volumes, supply chain disruptions, energy price increases, and the accelerating digitalisation of logistics have all reshaped the way the Company operates. At the same time, the labour market for logistics workers has tightened considerably, with warehouse operatives, dispatchers and professional drivers in particularly short supply.

### 1.3 Why now?

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, the question is no longer whether the world of work will change, but how quickly organisations are able to adapt. Several factors make this the right moment for Northwind to consider a four-day week:

- **Talent scarcity.** The logistics sector across Central Europe is facing a structural shortage of qualified staff. Competition for warehouse operatives, dispatchers and drivers is intense, and wage-based competition alone is no longer sufficient to attract and retain the best people.
- **Changing employee expectations.** Younger generations entering the workforce place a high value on flexibility, wellbeing and work-life balance. A four-day week is one of the most visible and powerful signals that an employer can send in this regard.
- **Productivity pressure.** Margins in freight forwarding and warehousing remain under pressure. Northwind needs to find new ways to increase productivity per employee and per hour worked, rather than relying on additional hours and overtime.
- **Wellbeing and burnout.** Our internal data shows rising levels of overtime, absenteeism and turnover, which are widely recognised as early indicators of burnout and disengagement.
- **Reputation and differentiation.** Very few logistics companies in our region have introduced a four-day week. Northwind has the opportunity to become a pioneer, with significant reputational benefits among both employees and customers.
- **Momentum in the evidence base.** The number and scale of four-day week pilots has grown substantially since 2019, and the evidence now available is considerably more robust than it was even a few years ago.

### 1.4 Strategic alignment

The four-day week initiative is closely aligned with Northwind's strategic priorities for 2026–2030, as defined in the Company's strategy document "Northwind 2030: Moving Forward Together". In particular, it supports the following strategic pillars:

| Strategic pillar | How the four-day week contributes |
|---|---|
| **People first** | Improves wellbeing, engagement and retention; positions Northwind as an employer of choice |
| **Operational excellence** | Encourages process optimisation, elimination of low-value activities and smarter scheduling |
| **Customer intimacy** | Engaged, rested employees deliver better service and build stronger customer relationships |
| **Sustainable growth** | Reduces turnover and recruitment costs; supports long-term capability building |
| **Responsible business** | Contributes to social sustainability goals and supports ESG reporting |

It is important to note that the four-day week should not be seen as a standalone HR initiative. Rather, it is a strategic, cross-functional transformation that touches every part of the organisation, from operations and customer service to finance and IT. A holistic approach is therefore essential.

### 1.5 Scope

This analysis covers all 120 employees of Northwind Logistics s.r.o. across all departments. It considers full-time employees only; the small number of part-time and temporary agency workers used during peak periods are addressed separately in Section 12. The analysis focuses on the Company's main site; however, the principles and recommendations are designed to be applicable to any future sites that Northwind may open.

## 2 Methodology

### 2.1 Overall approach

To ensure a robust, balanced and holistic assessment, the Strategy & Transformation Office adopted a multi-method approach combining desk research, internal data analysis, employee engagement and financial modelling. This approach was designed to ensure that the recommendation is grounded in both external evidence and the specific realities of Northwind's business.

The analysis was conducted over a period of approximately seven weeks between late August and mid-October 2026 and consisted of five main workstreams:

1. **Literature and evidence review** — a review of published pilot results, academic studies, industry reports and media coverage relating to four-day week initiatives globally, with a particular focus on logistics, transport and operationally intensive industries.
2. **Internal data analysis** — analysis of Northwind's HR, payroll, operational and customer data for the period January 2023 to August 2026, including headcount, working hours, overtime, absenteeism, turnover, recruitment costs and SLA performance.
3. **Employee engagement** — an anonymous online survey of all employees, complemented by six focus groups and individual interviews with department heads.
4. **Operational modelling** — the design and assessment of alternative four-day week models against Northwind's operational requirements, including shift patterns and customer coverage.
5. **Financial modelling** — the development of a financial model to estimate the costs, savings and net benefit of the four-day week under the recommended model.

### 2.2 Literature and evidence review

The literature review covered more than 60 sources, including pilot programme reports, peer-reviewed academic papers, think-tank publications, industry association reports, and reputable media coverage. Sources were selected based on their relevance to Northwind's context, the size and quality of the underlying data, and their recency. Where possible, priority was given to studies including companies from the logistics, transport, warehousing and manufacturing sectors.

It is important to note that the evidence base on the four-day week is still developing, and that many pilots have been conducted in office-based, knowledge-work settings. However, as described in Section 5, an increasing body of evidence is now available specifically for logistics and operational environments, which significantly strengthens the relevance of the findings for Northwind.

### 2.3 Internal data analysis

Internal data was extracted from Northwind's HR information system, the payroll system, the warehouse management system (WMS), the transport management system (TMS), and the customer service ticketing system. Data was cleaned, consolidated and analysed by the Strategy & Transformation Office with support from the Finance and IT departments.

Key data sets included:

- Headcount by department, contract type and role
- Contracted and actual hours worked, including overtime
- Absenteeism (sick leave and other unplanned absence)
- Voluntary and involuntary turnover
- Recruitment volumes, time-to-hire and cost-per-hire
- Warehouse throughput (pallets, order lines and picks per hour)
- Transport performance (on-time delivery, utilisation)
- Customer SLA performance and complaint volumes

### 2.4 Employee survey and focus groups

An anonymous online survey was distributed to all 120 employees between 8 and 22 September 2026. The survey was available in both Czech and English and could be completed on a mobile phone, on shared terminals in the warehouse canteen, or on a desktop computer. A total of 97 employees completed the survey, representing a response rate of 81%, which is considered exceptionally high and provides a robust basis for the findings.

In addition, six focus groups were held with a cross-section of employees from the warehouse, dispatch, drivers, customer service, and office functions. Each focus group included between six and nine participants and lasted approximately 60 minutes. Individual interviews were also conducted with all department heads and with members of the Executive Board. The full list of survey questions is included in Appendix B.

### 2.5 Operational and financial modelling

The operational modelling workstream assessed five alternative models for introducing a four-day week (described in Section 7), each evaluated against a set of criteria including customer coverage, operational feasibility, employee preference, cost and complexity.

The financial model was developed in close cooperation with the Finance department. It estimates the one-off implementation costs, the recurring costs, and the recurring savings associated with the recommended model over a five-year horizon. Assumptions were based on internal data, on external benchmarks drawn from the literature review, and on the judgement of the project team. The financial model is described in detail in Section 8.

### 2.6 Limitations

As with any analysis of this kind, there are certain limitations that should be acknowledged. The literature on the four-day week is still evolving, and results from one context cannot always be directly transferred to another. Survey results reflect employees' stated preferences and expectations, which may differ from their actual behaviour. Financial estimates are inherently based on assumptions and should be revisited as real data from the pilot becomes available. Nevertheless, the project team is confident that the analysis provides a sound and balanced basis for the Board's decision, and that the overall direction of the findings is clear.
