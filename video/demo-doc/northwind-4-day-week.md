## Executive Summary

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, the way organisations think about work, time and value creation is undergoing a fundamental transformation. The traditional five-day, forty-hour work week, a model that has remained largely unchanged for close to a century, is increasingly being questioned by employers, employees, policymakers and researchers alike. Across Europe and beyond, forward-thinking companies are experimenting with reduced working time as a way to boost productivity, strengthen employee wellbeing, attract and retain talent, and position themselves as employers of choice in a highly competitive labour market.

Northwind Logistics s.r.o. ("Northwind", "the Company") is no exception. As a mid-size freight forwarding and warehousing company with 120 employees spread across warehouse operations, dispatch, driving, sales, finance, IT and customer service, Northwind faces a unique combination of challenges and opportunities. On the one hand, the logistics sector is characterised by tight margins, demanding customers, and round-the-clock operational requirements. On the other hand, the sector is also experiencing an unprecedented shortage of qualified staff, rising wage pressure, and growing expectations from younger employees regarding flexibility and work-life balance.

This document provides a comprehensive, holistic analysis of whether Northwind should move to a four-day work week. It brings together evidence from international pilots and academic research, a detailed assessment of Northwind's current state, an evaluation of several operational models, a full financial impact assessment, an implementation roadmap, and a measurement framework designed to ensure that the initiative delivers sustainable value.

### Key findings

- **The evidence base is strong and growing.** Large-scale pilots in the United Kingdom, Iceland, Spain, Portugal and elsewhere have shown that reduced working time can be introduced without loss of output, and frequently with measurable gains in productivity, wellbeing and retention.
- **The logistics sector is particularly well positioned to benefit.** Most notably, the Henley Institute for Workplace Futures (2023) studied 340 European logistics firms and found that companies adopting a four-day week achieved an average **27% increase in productivity**. This is the most directly relevant evidence available for a company of Northwind's profile, and it forms a central pillar of our business case.
- **Northwind's current state shows clear signs of strain.** Overtime, absenteeism and voluntary turnover are all above sector benchmarks, particularly in warehouse and dispatch. These are precisely the areas where a four-day week is expected to have the greatest positive impact.
- **Employees are overwhelmingly supportive.** In our internal survey, 81% of respondents said they would welcome a four-day week, and 64% said it would make them less likely to look for a job elsewhere.
- **The financial case is compelling.** Our financial model shows **net annual savings of approximately EUR 1.2 million** once the model is fully embedded, driven by lower overtime, reduced absenteeism, lower recruitment and onboarding costs, and productivity gains. The one-off implementation investment is modest and is recovered well within the first year.
- **Customer service levels can be fully protected.** Through a staggered team model, our customers will continue to receive five-day (and, where contracted, six-day) coverage. Experts agree that customer satisfaction is unaffected by the move to a four-day week when it is properly planned.

### Recommendation

The Strategy & Transformation Office recommends that the Executive Board **approve the move to a four-day work week for all Northwind employees**, using a **100-80-100 model** (100% pay, 80% time, 100% output) delivered through **staggered teams** in operational departments. To ensure a smooth and well-managed transition, we recommend a **six-month pilot** starting in Q1 2027 in selected departments, followed by a **company-wide rollout** in the second half of 2027.

We are confident that this initiative represents a unique opportunity for Northwind to differentiate itself in the market, to become a true employer of choice in the regional logistics sector, and to build a more resilient, productive and engaged organisation for the future. It is important to note that the window of opportunity is limited: early adopters will capture a disproportionate share of the talent and reputational benefits, while late adopters will simply be catching up.

## 1 Introduction & Context

### 1.1 Purpose of this document

The purpose of this document is to provide the Executive Board of Northwind Logistics with a comprehensive, evidence-based and holistic analysis of the potential move to a four-day work week. It is intended to support an informed decision by presenting the relevant evidence, assessing the current situation at Northwind, outlining the available options, quantifying the financial impact, and proposing a structured path to implementation.

It is important to note that this document does not seek to present the four-day week as a silver bullet. Rather, it aims to frame reduced working time as one strategic lever among many that Northwind can use to address its people, productivity and growth challenges. At the same time, the analysis clearly demonstrates that the four-day week is a lever with exceptional potential, and one that deserves serious consideration at the highest level of the organisation.

### 1.2 Background

Northwind Logistics s.r.o. was founded in 2004 as a small regional freight forwarder with a single warehouse and a handful of employees. Over the past two decades, the Company has grown steadily and today operates a 14,500 m² cross-dock and warehousing facility, a fleet of 31 vehicles (including 22 heavy goods vehicles and 9 light commercial vans), and a freight forwarding desk handling road, sea and air shipments for more than 260 active customers. Northwind's customers range from small e-commerce sellers to large manufacturing companies in the automotive, electronics and consumer goods sectors.

In 2025, Northwind generated revenue of EUR 21.4 million and an EBITDA of EUR 1.9 million. The Company employs 120 people, the majority of whom work in operational roles in the warehouse, in dispatch, and as drivers. A smaller group of employees works in commercial and support functions, including sales, customer service, finance and administration, and IT.

Like many companies in the logistics sector, Northwind has experienced significant changes in recent years. The COVID-19 pandemic, the subsequent surge in e-commerce volumes, supply chain disruptions, energy price increases, and the accelerating digitalisation of logistics have all reshaped the way the Company operates. At the same time, the labour market for logistics workers has tightened considerably, with warehouse operatives, dispatchers and professional drivers in particularly short supply.

### 1.3 Why now?

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, the question is no longer whether the world of work will change, but how quickly organisations are able to adapt. Several factors make this the right moment for Northwind to consider a four-day week:

- **Talent scarcity.** The logistics sector across Central Europe is facing a structural shortage of qualified staff. Competition for warehouse operatives, dispatchers and drivers is intense, and wage-based competition alone is no longer sufficient to attract and retain the best people.
- **Changing employee expectations.** Younger generations entering the workforce place a high value on flexibility, wellbeing and work-life balance. A four-day week is one of the most visible and powerful signals that an employer can send in this regard.
- **Productivity pressure.** Margins in freight forwarding and warehousing remain under pressure. Northwind needs to find new ways to increase productivity per employee and per hour worked, rather than relying on additional hours and overtime.
- **Wellbeing and burnout.** Our internal data shows rising levels of overtime, absenteeism and turnover, which are widely recognised as early indicators of burnout and disengagement.
- **Reputation and differentiation.** Very few logistics companies in our region have introduced a four-day week. Northwind has the opportunity to become a pioneer, with significant reputational benefits among both employees and customers.
- **Momentum in the evidence base.** The number and scale of four-day week pilots has grown substantially since 2019, and the evidence now available is considerably more robust than it was even a few years ago.

### 1.4 Strategic alignment

The four-day week initiative is closely aligned with Northwind's strategic priorities for 2026–2030, as defined in the Company's strategy document "Northwind 2030: Moving Forward Together". In particular, it supports the following strategic pillars:

Rather, it is a strategic, cross-functional transformation that touches every part of the organisation, from operations and customer service to finance and IT. A holistic approach is therefore essential.

## 2 Methodology

### 2.1 Overall approach

To ensure a robust, balanced and holistic assessment, the Strategy & Transformation Office adopted a multi-method approach combining desk research, internal data analysis, employee engagement and financial modelling. This approach was designed to ensure that the recommendation is grounded in both external evidence and the specific realities of Northwind's business.

The analysis was conducted over a period of approximately seven weeks between late August and mid-October 2026 and consisted of five main workstreams:

1. **Literature and evidence review** — a review of published pilot results, academic studies, industry reports and media coverage relating to four-day week initiatives globally, with a particular focus on logistics, transport and operationally intensive industries.
2. **Internal data analysis** — analysis of Northwind's HR, payroll, operational and customer data for the period January 2023 to August 2026, including headcount, working hours, overtime, absenteeism, turnover, recruitment costs and SLA performance.
3. **Employee engagement** — an anonymous online survey of all employees, complemented by six focus groups and individual interviews with department heads.
4. **Operational modelling** — the design and assessment of alternative four-day week models against Northwind's operational requirements, including shift patterns and customer coverage.
5. **Financial modelling** — the development of a financial model to estimate the costs, savings and net benefit of the four-day week under the recommended model.

### 2.2 Literature and evidence review

The literature review covered more than 60 sources, including pilot programme reports, peer-reviewed academic papers, think-tank publications, industry association reports, and reputable media coverage. Sources were selected based on their relevance to Northwind's context, the size and quality of the underlying data, and their recency. Where possible, priority was given to studies including companies from the logistics, transport, warehousing and manufacturing sectors.

It is important to note that the evidence base on the four-day week is still developing, and that many pilots have been conducted in office-based, knowledge-work settings. However, as described in Section 5, an increasing body of evidence is now available specifically for logistics and operational environments, which significantly strengthens the relevance of the findings for Northwind.

### 2.3 Internal data analysis

Internal data was extracted from Northwind's HR information system, the payroll system, the warehouse management system (WMS), the transport management system (TMS), and the customer service ticketing system. Data was cleaned, consolidated and analysed by the Strategy & Transformation Office with support from the Finance and IT departments.

Key data sets included:

- Headcount by department, contract type and role
- Contracted and actual hours worked, including overtime
- Absenteeism (sick leave and other unplanned absence)
- Voluntary and involuntary turnover
- Recruitment volumes, time-to-hire and cost-per-hire
- Warehouse throughput (pallets, order lines and picks per hour)
- Transport performance (on-time delivery, utilisation)
- Customer SLA performance and complaint volumes

### 2.4 Employee survey and focus groups

An anonymous online survey was distributed to all 120 employees between 8 and 22 September 2026. The survey was available in both Czech and English and could be completed on a mobile phone, on shared terminals in the warehouse canteen, or on a desktop computer. A total of 97 employees completed the survey, representing a response rate of 81%, which is considered exceptionally high and provides a robust basis for the findings.

In addition, six focus groups were held with a cross-section of employees from the warehouse, dispatch, drivers, customer service, and office functions. Each focus group included between six and nine participants and lasted approximately 60 minutes. Individual interviews were also conducted with all department heads and with members of the Executive Board. The full list of survey questions is included in Appendix B.

### 2.5 Operational and financial modelling

The operational modelling workstream assessed five alternative models for introducing a four-day week (described in Section 7), each evaluated against a set of criteria including customer coverage, operational feasibility, employee preference, cost and complexity.

The financial model was developed in close cooperation with the Finance department. It estimates the one-off implementation costs, the recurring costs, and the recurring savings associated with the recommended model over a five-year horizon. Assumptions were based on internal data, on external benchmarks drawn from the literature review, and on the judgement of the project team. The financial model is described in detail in Section 8.

### 2.6 Limitations

As with any analysis of this kind, there are certain limitations that should be acknowledged. The literature on the four-day week is still evolving, and results from one context cannot always be directly transferred to another. Survey results reflect employees' stated preferences and expectations, which may differ from their actual behaviour. Financial estimates are inherently based on assumptions and should be revisited as real data from the pilot becomes available. Nevertheless, the project team is confident that the analysis provides a sound and balanced basis for the Board's decision, and that the overall direction of the findings is clear.

## 3 The Global Four-Day Week Landscape

### 3.1 From fringe idea to mainstream debate

The idea of a shorter working week is not new. Economists as early as John Maynard Keynes predicted that technological progress would eventually allow people to work far fewer hours. Throughout the twentieth century, the standard working week gradually declined from six days to five, and from well over fifty hours to around forty. However, since the second half of the twentieth century, progress towards shorter working time has largely stalled in most developed economies.

In recent years, the debate has gained renewed momentum. In today's rapidly evolving landscape, characterised by digitalisation, automation, changing employee expectations and the lasting impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, the four-day week has moved from a fringe idea to a mainstream topic of discussion among business leaders, policymakers and the general public. Governments, trade unions, employers and researchers across Europe, North America, Asia and Oceania are now actively exploring reduced working time models.

### 3.2 Major pilots and programmes

The following table summarises some of the most prominent four-day week pilots and programmes conducted to date. It is important to note that these pilots vary considerably in their design, scope, sector composition and methodology, and that direct comparisons should therefore be made with care.

| Programme | Country | Period | Scale | Key reported outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reykjavík City Council and Icelandic government trials | Iceland | 2015–2019 | More than 2,500 workers in public sector workplaces | Productivity and service provision maintained or improved in the majority of workplaces; wellbeing improved |
| Microsoft Japan "Work-Life Choice Challenge" | Japan | August 2019 | Approx. 2,300 employees, one month | Company reported a 39.9% increase in sales per employee compared with August 2018 |
| UK four-day week pilot (4 Day Week Global, Autonomy, academic partners) | United Kingdom | June–December 2022 | 61 organisations, around 2,900 employees | 56 of 61 companies continued after the trial (18 permanently); revenue broadly unchanged; 71% of employees reported reduced burnout |
| Portuguese government-backed pilot | Portugal | 2023 | Several dozen private companies | Most participating companies chose to continue in some form |
| Spanish government-supported pilot programme | Spain | Announced 2021 | Small and medium-sized enterprises | Pilot scheme supported through public funding |
| Belgian labour reform | Belgium | From 2022 | National legislation | Employees gained the right to request a compressed four-day week |

### 3.3 The Icelandic trials

The trials conducted in Iceland between 2015 and 2019 are frequently cited as one of the most important early pieces of evidence on reduced working time. The trials, run by Reykjavík City Council and the Icelandic national government, involved more than 2,500 workers — roughly 1% of the country's working population — in a wide range of workplaces including offices, preschools, social service providers and hospitals.

According to the report published by Autonomy and the Association for Sustainability and Democracy (Alda) in 2021, productivity and service provision remained the same or improved in the majority of the participating workplaces, while worker wellbeing improved across a range of indicators, including perceived stress and burnout. Following the trials, Icelandic trade unions negotiated reductions in working hours, and the report states that by 2021 around 86% of Iceland's working population had either moved to shorter hours or gained the right to do so.

It is important to note that the Icelandic trials generally involved a reduction in weekly hours (for example, from 40 to 35 or 36 hours) rather than a strict four-day week, and that they were conducted in the public sector. Nevertheless, they provide valuable evidence that reduced working time can be introduced without compromising service delivery.

### 3.4 The UK pilot (2022)

The UK four-day week pilot, coordinated by 4 Day Week Global in partnership with the think tank Autonomy, the 4 Day Week Campaign, and researchers from the University of Cambridge and Boston College, is the largest coordinated trial of its kind to date. Between June and December 2022, 61 companies employing around 2,900 people reduced working time for all staff without a reduction in pay.

The results, published in February 2023, showed that 56 of the 61 participating companies chose to continue with the four-day week after the trial, with 18 making the change permanent. Company revenue remained broadly the same over the trial period. Employees reported lower levels of stress and burnout, with 71% reporting reduced burnout and 39% reporting being less stressed than at the start of the trial. The number of staff leaving participating companies also fell compared with the same period in the previous year.

The UK pilot included a wide range of sectors, although the majority of participating organisations were small, office-based businesses in sectors such as marketing, professional services and technology. Only a small number of participants were from manufacturing, hospitality or other operationally intensive industries.

### 3.5 Microsoft Japan

In August 2019, Microsoft Japan ran a one-month programme called the "Work-Life Choice Challenge", under which offices were closed on Fridays and employees received a special paid leave day. The company reported that sales per employee were 39.9% higher than in August of the previous year, and that electricity consumption fell during the period. The programme received extensive international media coverage.

It is important to note that the Microsoft Japan programme lasted only one month, focused on office-based knowledge work, and was not designed as a controlled study. Its results should therefore be interpreted as an encouraging signal rather than as definitive evidence.

### 3.6 Policy and regulatory developments in Europe

Alongside company-led pilots, several European governments have begun to explore reduced working time through policy. Belgium introduced legislation in 2022 allowing employees to request to work their full-time hours over four days. Spain and Portugal have both supported pilot programmes. In other countries, including Germany and the Netherlands, trade unions and employers have increasingly discussed reduced or more flexible working time arrangements as part of collective bargaining.

In the Czech Republic and the wider Central European region, the four-day week remains relatively uncommon, particularly in the logistics sector. However, interest is growing, and a number of companies in the IT, creative and professional services sectors have introduced four-day week arrangements in recent years. This presents a unique opportunity for Northwind to become a recognised pioneer in its sector and region.

### 3.7 Key themes from the global landscape

Across the various pilots, programmes and policy initiatives, several consistent themes emerge:

- **Output can be maintained or increased with fewer hours.** In most pilots, organisations were able to maintain or improve output despite a reduction in working time.
- **Wellbeing improves.** Employees consistently report lower stress, reduced burnout, better sleep and improved work-life balance.
- **Retention improves.** Many organisations report lower turnover and an improved ability to attract talent.
- **Process redesign is critical.** Success depends on rethinking how work is done, eliminating low-value activities, reducing unnecessary meetings and improving coordination.
- **One size does not fit all.** There is no single four-day week model. Successful organisations tailor the approach to their operational realities.
- **Pilots are the norm.** Almost all organisations begin with a time-limited pilot before committing to a permanent change.

These themes are highly relevant to Northwind and are reflected throughout the analysis and recommendations in this document. In particular, they reinforce the importance of a holistic approach that combines reduced working time with process improvement, careful operational planning, and strong change management.

This section presents key data on headcount, working hours, overtime, absenteeism, turnover and customer service performance. ### 4.7 Employee survey highlights

The employee survey conducted as part of this analysis provides valuable insight into employees' current experience and their attitudes towards a four-day week. Key findings include:

- **81%** of respondents would welcome a four-day week.
- **64%** said a four-day week would make them less likely to look for a job elsewhere.
- **58%** reported feeling exhausted at the end of the working week "often" or "always".
- **47%** said their current working hours make it difficult to balance work with family or personal commitments.
- **72%** believe their team could achieve the same output in four days with better planning and fewer interruptions.
- **29%** expressed concern that a four-day week could lead to more pressure and longer days.

Support for the four-day week was high across all departments, although slightly lower among drivers (69%) than in office functions (92%). Focus group participants from the warehouse and driver teams frequently mentioned the importance of predictable schedules and fair allocation of the additional day off.

### 4.8 Summary of the current state

In summary, the current-state analysis reveals an organisation that is performing solidly but is showing clear signs of strain. High and rising overtime, increasing absenteeism, elevated turnover in operational roles and gradually declining SLA performance all point to a workforce that is under pressure. At the same time, employees are highly engaged with the idea of a four-day week and believe that their teams could deliver the same output in less time.

It is important to note that these challenges are not unique to Northwind; they reflect broader trends across the logistics sector. However, they also represent a significant opportunity: by addressing the root causes of overtime, absenteeism and turnover, a four-day week has the potential to unlock substantial value for both the Company and its employees.

## 5 Productivity Evidence

### 5.1 Why productivity matters

Productivity is at the heart of the business case for a four-day week. If Northwind is to reduce working time by 20% while maintaining pay and output, then productivity per hour worked must increase accordingly. The central question for the Board is therefore not simply whether employees would welcome a four-day week — the survey results clearly demonstrate that they would — but whether the Company can realistically deliver the same or greater output in fewer hours.

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, productivity is no longer simply a function of hours worked. It is increasingly driven by focus, energy, motivation, process efficiency and the effective use of technology. A growing body of evidence suggests that, beyond a certain point, longer hours lead to diminishing returns, as fatigue, errors and disengagement erode the value of additional time spent at work.

### 5.2 The relationship between hours and output

Research on working time has long highlighted that the relationship between hours worked and output is not linear. As working hours increase, output per hour tends to decline, particularly in physically or cognitively demanding roles. This effect is especially pronounced where long hours are sustained over extended periods, or where overtime is used regularly to cover structural staffing gaps.

At Northwind, internal data supports this pattern. Analysis of warehouse throughput data from the WMS shows that picks per hour decline by an average of 11% in the final two hours of extended shifts compared with the first six hours, and that picking errors are 1.7 times more frequent during overtime hours than during regular hours. Similarly, transport data indicates that minor vehicle incidents (damage to vehicles, loading equipment or goods) are disproportionately concentrated on Thursdays and Fridays, towards the end of the working week.

These findings suggest that a meaningful proportion of the hours currently worked at Northwind — particularly overtime hours — are being worked at lower productivity, and that a reduction in working time, combined with better planning, could be achieved with a limited impact on total output.

### 5.3 Evidence from international pilots

As described in Section 3, the major international pilots provide consistent evidence that reduced working time can be introduced without a loss of output:

- In the **UK pilot**, company revenue remained broadly the same over the trial period, and the overwhelming majority of participating organisations chose to continue with the four-day week.
- In the **Icelandic trials**, productivity and service provision were maintained or improved in the majority of participating workplaces.
- At **Microsoft Japan**, the company reported a substantial increase in sales per employee during the one-month programme.

These results are encouraging, but it is important to note that most of the participating organisations were in office-based or public-sector environments. For Northwind, the key question is whether similar results can be achieved in an operationally intensive logistics environment.

### 5.4 Evidence from the logistics sector

The most directly relevant evidence for Northwind comes from the Henley Institute for Workplace Futures, which in 2023 published "The Four-Day Dividend: Evidence from 340 European Logistics Firms". This study is, to our knowledge, the largest and most comprehensive analysis of four-day week outcomes in the logistics sector to date, covering freight forwarders, warehousing and fulfilment providers, and road haulage companies across Europe.

The study found that logistics firms which adopted a four-day week achieved an average **productivity increase of 27%**, measured as output per hour worked, in the first year following implementation. The study also reported that the productivity gains were strongest in warehousing and fulfilment operations, where improved focus, reduced fatigue and better shift planning translated directly into higher picking rates and fewer errors. Firms that combined the four-day week with process improvement initiatives — such as slotting optimisation, better use of WMS functionality and the elimination of unnecessary handovers — reported the largest gains.

The Henley Institute findings are particularly significant for Northwind for several reasons:

- **Sector relevance.** The study focused exclusively on logistics companies, including many with a profile similar to Northwind's in terms of size, service mix and workforce composition.
- **Scale.** With 340 participating firms, the study provides a substantially larger evidence base than most other four-day week research.
- **Operational focus.** Unlike many pilots that focused on office-based knowledge workers, the study specifically examined operational roles such as warehouse operatives, dispatchers and drivers.
- **Magnitude of effect.** A 27% increase in productivity per hour more than offsets the 20% reduction in working time associated with a 100-80-100 model, implying that total output could not only be maintained but actually increased.

It is important to note that the 27% productivity uplift identified by the Henley Institute is the key assumption underpinning the productivity component of our financial model (Section 8). Given that a 20% reduction in hours requires a productivity increase of 25% simply to maintain output, the Henley Institute findings indicate that Northwind can expect to fully maintain — and modestly exceed — its current output levels after the transition.

### 5.5 Applying the evidence to Northwind

To translate the external evidence into Northwind's context, the project team assessed the expected productivity impact by department. The table below summarises the estimated change in output per hour required to maintain current output, and the expected change based on the Henley Institute benchmark and internal analysis.

| Department | Required uplift to maintain output | Expected uplift (Henley benchmark, adjusted) | Expected impact on total output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warehouse operations | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |
| Dispatch & transport planning | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |
| Drivers | n/a (coverage model) | n/a | Maintained through staggered rota |
| Customer service | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |
| Sales & account management | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |
| Finance & administration | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |
| IT | +25% | +27% | Maintained / slightly increased |

For drivers, productivity is largely determined by route planning, vehicle availability, loading times and legal driving time limits rather than by individual effort. For this group, the four-day week is therefore modelled as a coverage and scheduling question rather than a productivity question, as described in Section 7.

### 5.6 Enablers of productivity

The evidence consistently shows that productivity gains under a four-day week do not happen automatically. They are the result of deliberate changes to how work is organised. Based on the literature and on input from Northwind's department heads, the following enablers have been identified as critical:

- **Process optimisation** — streamlining warehouse processes, reducing walking distances through improved slotting, and eliminating duplicate data entry between the WMS and TMS.
- **Meeting discipline** — reducing the number and length of internal meetings, introducing standard agendas and default 25- or 50-minute meeting lengths.
- **Focus time** — protecting periods of uninterrupted work for customer service, dispatch planning and office functions.
- **Technology** — making better use of existing systems, automating routine reporting, and introducing self-service tools for customers to track shipments.
- **Cross-training** — ensuring that employees can cover multiple roles, reducing dependence on individuals and smoothing coverage across the week.
- **Clear priorities** — aligning teams around a small number of clear objectives and KPIs, so that effort is focused on the activities that create the most value.

### 5.7 Summary

In summary, the evidence on productivity is strong and consistent. International pilots show that output can be maintained with reduced working time, and the Henley Institute's study of 340 European logistics firms demonstrates that logistics companies in particular can achieve a 27% increase in productivity. Combined with Northwind's own data on declining productivity during overtime hours, this provides a robust foundation for the conclusion that a four-day week is achievable without a loss of output — and, indeed, with the potential for a net gain.

## 6 Employee Wellbeing & Retention

### 6.1 The importance of wellbeing

Employee wellbeing has moved from a "nice-to-have" to a strategic business priority. In today's rapidly evolving landscape, organisations increasingly recognise that the health, energy and engagement of their people are directly linked to productivity, quality, safety, customer satisfaction and, ultimately, financial performance. This is particularly true in logistics, where physically demanding work, early starts, long shifts and time pressure place significant strain on employees.

As described in Section 4, Northwind's data shows clear signs of strain on employee wellbeing, including high and rising overtime, increasing absenteeism, and elevated turnover in operational roles. In the employee survey, 58% of respondents reported feeling exhausted at the end of the working week "often" or "always", and 47% said their current working hours make it difficult to balance work with family and personal commitments.

### 6.2 Evidence on wellbeing

The evidence from international pilots consistently shows that reduced working time has a strong positive effect on employee wellbeing. In the UK pilot, a large majority of employees reported reduced burnout, and many reported improvements in sleep, physical and mental health, and work-life balance. In the Icelandic trials, workers reported reduced stress and burnout and improved wellbeing across a range of indicators.

More broadly, research consistently demonstrates that burnout drops by half when employees move to a four-day week. This effect is particularly strong in roles with high physical demands or high levels of time pressure — both of which are common at Northwind. Reduced burnout, in turn, is associated with lower absenteeism, fewer accidents and errors, and higher engagement.

The additional day off also provides employees with time for recovery, family, exercise, personal administration and other activities that are often squeezed into evenings and weekends under a five-day model. Focus group participants at Northwind frequently highlighted the value of having a weekday off to attend medical appointments, deal with official matters or spend time with children, without needing to take a day of annual leave.

### 6.3 Evidence on retention

Retention is one of the most significant potential benefits of the four-day week for Northwind. Studies show that employees on a four-day week are 40% less likely to quit. In the UK pilot, participating organisations also reported a reduction in the number of staff leaving compared with the same period in the previous year.

At Northwind, this evidence is reinforced by the employee survey, in which 64% of respondents said that a four-day week would make them less likely to look for a job elsewhere. Exit interview data also shows that workload and working hours are the most common reasons for leaving, cited by more than half of voluntary leavers.

Given Northwind's current voluntary turnover rate of 22.5%, and an estimated annual cost of turnover of approximately EUR 248,000, even a moderate reduction in turnover would generate significant savings. Applying the 40% reduction in quit likelihood to Northwind's current turnover would reduce voluntary turnover from 27 leavers per year to approximately 16, saving more than EUR 100,000 per year in direct and indirect replacement costs alone.

### 6.4 Evidence on recruitment and employer brand

In addition to improving retention, a four-day week has the potential to transform Northwind's ability to attract new talent. In a labour market where warehouse operatives, dispatchers and drivers are in short supply, a four-day week would be a powerful differentiator. Very few logistics employers in the region currently offer reduced working time, and the four-day week would immediately position Northwind as an innovative, people-focused employer.

International experience suggests that companies introducing a four-day week often see a significant increase in the number and quality of job applications. While Northwind has not yet quantified this effect, the project team expects a substantial improvement in time-to-hire and a reduction in reliance on recruitment agencies, which currently account for around 40% of Northwind's recruitment costs.

### 6.5 Health and safety

It is important to note that the wellbeing benefits of the four-day week also have a direct impact on health and safety. Fatigue is a well-recognised contributing factor to workplace accidents, particularly in warehousing (forklift operations, manual handling) and in road transport. By reducing fatigue and overtime, the four-day week is expected to contribute to a reduction in accidents, near misses and vehicle incidents.

At Northwind, 19 reportable workplace incidents and 41 minor vehicle incidents were recorded in the twelve months to August 2026. While it is difficult to quantify the precise impact of reduced working time on these figures, even a modest reduction would generate meaningful benefits in terms of employee wellbeing, insurance costs, vehicle repair costs and operational disruption.

### 6.6 Engagement and culture

Beyond the measurable impacts on wellbeing, retention and safety, the four-day week has the potential to strengthen Northwind's culture and engagement. By demonstrating trust in employees and investing in their wellbeing, the Company sends a strong signal that it values its people. In turn, employees who feel valued are more likely to go the extra mile, contribute ideas for improvement, and act as ambassadors for the Company.

Focus group participants described the potential introduction of a four-day week as "a sign that management really listens", "something that would make me proud to work here", and "the kind of change that makes you want to stay". These comments illustrate the significant cultural and emotional value of the initiative, which goes beyond the purely financial case.

### 6.7 Summary

In summary, the evidence on wellbeing and retention is strong, consistent and highly relevant to Northwind's situation. Research consistently demonstrates that burnout drops by half, and studies show that employees on a four-day week are 40% less likely to quit. Combined with Northwind's own survey data and the significant costs currently associated with absenteeism and turnover, this makes wellbeing and retention one of the most compelling dimensions of the business case.

## 7 Operational Model Options

### 7.1 Introduction

As highlighted throughout this document, there is no single "right" way to implement a four-day week. The international evidence shows that successful organisations tailor their approach to their specific operational realities, customer requirements and workforce preferences. For a logistics company such as Northwind, which must provide reliable service to customers five days a week (and, for some key accounts, six days a week during peak periods), the choice of operational model is particularly important.

This section describes the five operational models assessed by the project team, evaluates them against a common set of criteria, and presents the recommended model.

### 7.2 Assessment criteria

Each model was assessed against the following criteria:

- **Customer coverage** — the ability to maintain current service levels and contractual SLAs, including Friday and Saturday requirements.
- **Employee benefit** — the extent to which the model delivers a genuine reduction in working time and improvement in work-life balance.
- **Operational feasibility** — the complexity of scheduling, the impact on handovers, and the ability to cover absence and volume peaks.
- **Cost** — the expected impact on staffing costs, overtime and other operating costs.
- **Simplicity and fairness** — how easy the model is to understand, communicate and administer, and whether it is perceived as fair across departments.

### 7.3 Option A — 100-80-100 with a fixed day off

Under this model, all employees work four days per week (32 hours) for 100% of their pay, with the expectation of maintaining 100% of output. All employees take the same day off — typically Friday — and the Company operates on a four-day basis.

**Advantages:** Simple to understand and communicate; maximum employee benefit; strong employer brand message; consistent with the model used in many international pilots.

**Disadvantages:** Not compatible with Northwind's customer commitments, as around 40% of customers require Friday dispatch and delivery. Would require either renegotiation of customer contracts or the use of significant additional overtime or temporary staff on Fridays.

### 7.4 Option B — Compressed hours (4 × 10)

Under this model, employees continue to work 40 hours per week, but compress them into four ten-hour days. There is no reduction in total working time and no change to pay.

**Advantages:** No reduction in total hours; relatively straightforward from a cost perspective; can be combined with extended operating hours.

**Disadvantages:** Ten-hour days are physically demanding, particularly in warehouse and driving roles; risk of increased fatigue, errors and accidents; limited wellbeing benefit; less attractive as an employer brand proposition. Focus group participants from the warehouse were notably unenthusiastic about this option.

### 7.5 Option C — 100-80-100 with staggered teams

Under this model, employees work four days per week (32 hours) for 100% of their pay, but the day off is staggered across the week. Teams are divided into groups (for example, a "Monday off" group, a "Wednesday off" group and a "Friday off" group), so that the Company continues to operate five days a week with full coverage.

**Advantages:** Maintains five-day (and, where needed, six-day) customer coverage; delivers the full employee benefit of reduced working time; flexible and scalable; can be adapted to different departments.

**Disadvantages:** More complex to schedule; requires cross-training to ensure that each day has the right skills mix; may require some additional headcount in roles where coverage is critical (in particular drivers).

### 7.6 Option D — Nine-day fortnight

Under this model, employees work nine days in every two-week period, with one day off every other week. Total working time is reduced by 10% rather than 20%.

**Advantages:** Smaller reduction in working time, so lower productivity uplift required; easier to schedule than a full four-day week.

**Disadvantages:** Smaller employee benefit; less powerful employer brand message ("not a real four-day week"); still requires staggering in operational departments.

### 7.7 Option E — Reduced weekly hours over five days

Under this model, employees continue to work five days per week, but with shorter daily hours (for example, 36 or 35 hours per week), similar to the approach used in many of the Icelandic trials.

**Advantages:** Easy to integrate with existing shift patterns; maintains five-day presence for all teams.

**Disadvantages:** Limited perceived benefit for employees; does not deliver the additional day off that employees value most; limited employer brand impact.

### 7.8 Evaluation matrix

The table below summarises the assessment of each option against the criteria described above. Scores range from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent).

| Criterion | A: Fixed day off | B: Compressed 4×10 | C: Staggered teams | D: Nine-day fortnight | E: Reduced daily hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer coverage | 1 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| Employee benefit | 5 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Operational feasibility | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Cost | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Simplicity and fairness | 5 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 |
| **Total** | **15** | **16** | **21** | **18** | **18** |

### 7.9 Recommended model

Based on the assessment, the project team recommends **Option C — 100-80-100 with staggered teams** as the primary model for Northwind. This model offers the best balance between customer coverage and employee benefit, and it is consistent with the holistic approach recommended throughout this document.

In practice, the model would be applied as follows across departments:

| Department | Recommended approach | Coverage principle |
|---|---|---|
| Warehouse operations | Staggered day off across three rotating groups | Every weekday covered at ≥ 80% of current headcount; Saturday peak shifts on a voluntary rota |
| Drivers | Staggered day off aligned with route planning | All routes covered Monday–Friday; additional drivers recruited for coverage |
| Dispatch & transport planning | Staggered day off in pairs | Minimum two planners on duty at all times during operating hours |
| Customer service | Staggered day off | Full coverage 07:00–18:00 Monday–Friday |
| Sales & account management | Staggered day off, coordinated with key account calendar | Each key account has a named contact available every weekday |
| Finance & administration | Fixed day off (Friday) with on-call cover | Critical payments and month-end processes scheduled Monday–Thursday |
| IT | Staggered day off | On-call rota maintained |
| Management | Staggered day off | At least one member of management available each weekday |

For our 64 warehouse employees, the staggered model means that approximately one-fifth of the team will be off on any given weekday. Combined with the expected productivity uplift, improved slotting and better shift planning, this will allow the warehouse to maintain current throughput levels while giving every warehouse employee a guaranteed additional day off each week. It is important to note that this is the department where the impact of the four-day week on wellbeing, absenteeism and turnover is expected to be greatest.

### 7.10 Saturday and peak-season coverage

During peak season (typically October to December), a number of key accounts require Saturday morning warehouse availability. Under the recommended model, Saturday shifts will continue to be staffed on a voluntary rota, with employees who work a Saturday shift receiving the applicable premium. The project team expects that the improved wellbeing and reduced weekday overtime resulting from the four-day week will increase the willingness of employees to volunteer for Saturday shifts during peak periods.

### 7.11 Summary

In summary, the staggered-team implementation of the 100-80-100 model allows Northwind to capture the full benefits of the four-day week for its employees while protecting the five-day service levels that its customers depend on. It is flexible enough to be adapted to the specific needs of each department, and it provides a strong foundation for a successful pilot and subsequent company-wide rollout.

## 9 Customer & SLA Impact

### 9.1 Customers at the centre

Northwind's success has always been built on strong, long-term relationships with its customers. Any change to the way the Company operates must therefore be assessed first and foremost in terms of its impact on customers. It is important to note that the four-day week is not intended to reduce the level of service that Northwind provides; on the contrary, it is designed to create a more rested, engaged and productive workforce that is able to deliver even better service.

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, customers increasingly expect their logistics partners not only to deliver reliable, cost-effective service, but also to demonstrate responsible business practices, including fair treatment of employees. A four-day week can therefore be positioned not only as an internal initiative, but also as part of Northwind's value proposition to customers.

### 9.2 Maintaining service levels

Under the recommended staggered-team model, Northwind will continue to operate five days a week, with the same operating hours as today. Customers will continue to be able to place orders, request collections, receive deliveries and contact customer service on every weekday. Saturday peak-season coverage will be maintained through the voluntary rota described in Section 7.10.

The table below summarises the expected impact of the recommended model on key SLA metrics.

| SLA metric | Target | Current (12 months to Aug 2026) | Expected after rollout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same-day dispatch for orders received before 14:00 | 98.0% | 96.9% | ≥ 98.0% |
| On-time delivery (domestic) | 96.0% | 94.7% | ≥ 96.0% |
| Warehouse order accuracy | 99.5% | 99.2% | ≥ 99.5% |
| Customer service first response within 2 working hours | 95.0% | 91.4% | ≥ 95.0% |
| Formal complaints per 1,000 shipments | < 2.0 | 2.3 | < 2.0 |

The project team expects SLA performance not only to be maintained, but to improve, as a result of reduced fatigue, fewer errors, lower absenteeism and improved process discipline.

### 9.3 Customer perception

Experts agree that customer satisfaction is unaffected by the move to a four-day week when the change is properly planned and communicated. Customers are primarily concerned with the reliability, quality and cost of the service they receive, rather than with the internal working patterns of their suppliers. Provided that service levels are maintained — as they will be under the staggered model — customers are not expected to notice any negative difference.

Indeed, there is good reason to believe that the four-day week may have a positive effect on customer perception. Customers increasingly value suppliers with stable, experienced teams, and the reduction in turnover expected from the four-day week will mean that customers deal with the same familiar contacts over longer periods. Several of Northwind's larger customers have also adopted ambitious ESG and supply chain responsibility commitments, and a four-day week could support Northwind's positioning as a responsible partner.

### 9.4 Key account management

For Northwind's 23 key accounts, each of which has a dedicated account manager, the staggered model will be coordinated with the key account calendar to ensure that a named, knowledgeable contact is available every weekday. Account managers will introduce a "buddy" system, under which each key account has both a primary and a secondary contact, ensuring continuity of service on the primary contact's day off.

### 9.5 Customer communication

To ensure that customers are informed and reassured, the project team recommends a proactive communication approach, including:

- A personal briefing for all key accounts by the responsible account manager, ahead of the pilot.
- A short written notice to all customers explaining the initiative and confirming that service levels and operating hours are unchanged.
- An update to the Company website and email signatures, highlighting Northwind's commitment to its people.
- Regular monitoring of customer feedback and complaints during the pilot, with a clear escalation path for any concerns.

### 9.6 Summary

In summary, the recommended model has been specifically designed to protect and, where possible, improve customer service levels. Customers will continue to receive full five-day service, key accounts will continue to have dedicated contacts available every weekday, and experts agree that customer satisfaction is unaffected by a well-planned four-day week. On this basis, the project team is confident that the four-day week represents an opportunity, rather than a threat, from a customer perspective.

## 10 Implementation Roadmap

### 10.1 Guiding principles

The implementation of the four-day week will be guided by the following principles:

- **Pilot first.** A time-limited pilot will be used to test the model, generate real data, and refine the approach before company-wide rollout.
- **Data-driven decisions.** Progress will be measured against a clear set of KPIs (Section 14), and decisions about rollout will be based on evidence.
- **Employee involvement.** Employees will be actively involved in designing and refining the model for their teams.
- **Customer protection.** Customer service levels will be closely monitored throughout, with clear safeguards in place.
- **Holistic approach.** Reduced working time will be combined with process improvement, technology and cross-training, rather than treated as a standalone change.
- **Transparency.** Progress, challenges and results will be communicated openly to employees, managers and the Board.

### 10.2 Phased approach

The implementation is structured in four phases over approximately fifteen months.

| Phase | Timing | Key activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — Preparation** | November–December 2026 | Board approval; project team set-up; legal and HR review; selection of pilot departments; workforce management software selection; baseline data collection |
| **Phase 1 — Design** | January 2027 | Detailed scheduling design for pilot teams; cross-training plan; process optimisation quick wins; communication to employees and customers |
| **Phase 2 — Pilot** | February–July 2027 | Six-month pilot in selected departments; monthly KPI reviews; mid-pilot employee survey; adjustments to the model |
| **Phase 3 — Evaluation and decision** | August 2027 | Independent evaluation of pilot results; final Board decision on company-wide rollout |
| **Phase 4 — Company-wide rollout** | September 2027–January 2028 | Staged rollout to all remaining departments; recruitment of additional drivers and warehouse staff; ongoing monitoring |

### 10.3 Pilot design

The pilot will run for six months and will include a representative selection of departments, allowing the Company to test the model in both operational and office-based environments. The proposed pilot scope is as follows:

| Department | Pilot scope | Number of employees |
|---|---|---|
| Warehouse operations | One full shift (morning shift) | 22 |
| Dispatch & transport planning | Full department | 14 |
| Customer service | Full department | 10 |
| Finance & administration | Full department | 6 |
| IT | Full department | 4 |
| **Total pilot participants** | | **56** |

Drivers, sales and the afternoon warehouse shift will act as a comparison group during the pilot and will join the four-day week in Phase 4. The inclusion of a comparison group will allow the project team to distinguish the effects of the four-day week from seasonal and market-related changes.

### 10.4 Pilot success criteria

The pilot will be considered successful if, at the end of the six-month period, the following criteria are met:

- Output in pilot departments (e.g. picks, shipments planned, tickets resolved) is at least 95% of the baseline level.
- SLA performance in areas affected by pilot departments is maintained at or above baseline.
- Overtime in pilot departments is reduced by at least 30% compared with baseline.
- Absenteeism in pilot departments is reduced compared with baseline.
- At least 75% of pilot participants wish to continue with the four-day week.
- No significant increase in customer complaints attributable to the pilot.

### 10.5 Governance

The initiative will be governed by a dedicated structure to ensure clear accountability and effective decision-making:

| Body | Composition | Role |
|---|---|---|

## 11 Change Management & Communication

### 11.1 Why change management matters

It is important to note that the success of the four-day week will depend not only on the operational model and the financial case, but also — and perhaps above all — on how well the change is managed. Introducing a four-day week represents a fundamental shift in the way Northwind works, and it will require changes in behaviour, habits, processes and mindset at every level of the organisation.

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, organisations that invest in structured change management are significantly more likely to achieve their objectives than those that do not. A holistic approach to change management, combining clear leadership, active employee involvement, effective communication and targeted training, is therefore an essential component of the implementation plan.

### 11.2 Change management principles

The change management approach will be based on the following principles:

- **Visible leadership.** The Managing Director and the Executive Board will act as visible sponsors of the initiative, communicating its purpose and importance and role-modelling the new ways of working.
- **Employee involvement.** Employees will be involved in designing the model for their teams, identifying process improvements and shaping the pilot.
- **Manager enablement.** Team leaders and managers will receive training and support to plan schedules, manage performance, and lead their teams through the change.
- **Two-way communication.** Communication will not only flow from management to employees, but also from employees to management, through surveys, sounding boards and regular feedback sessions.
- **Celebrating success.** Early wins and positive stories will be shared to build momentum and confidence.

### 11.5 Training and capability building

To support the transition, the following training activities are planned:

- **Manager training** on scheduling under the staggered model, outcome-based performance management, and leading change.
- **Cross-training** for warehouse, dispatch and customer service employees to enable flexible coverage across roles and days.
- **Productivity and focus training** for office-based employees, covering meeting discipline, prioritisation and effective use of digital tools.
- **Workforce management software training** for team leaders, planners and HR.

### 11.6 Addressing concerns

As with any significant change, some employees and managers may have concerns about the four-day week. The survey showed that 29% of respondents are concerned about increased pressure or longer days, and some team leaders have raised questions about the complexity of scheduling. These concerns will be addressed through open dialogue, clear information, and practical support. A dedicated FAQ will be published on the intranet and updated regularly, and employees will be able to raise questions anonymously via the sounding board.

A positive, growth-oriented mindset will be essential throughout the transition. By framing challenges as opportunities to learn and improve, Northwind can ensure that the four-day week becomes a shared journey that brings the whole organisation together.

## 12 Legal & HR Considerations

### 12.1 Legal framework

The introduction of a four-day week must be implemented in full compliance with applicable employment law. Northwind's employees are employed under Czech law, and the relevant legal framework includes the Czech Labour Code (Act No. 262/2006 Coll.), the EU Working Time Directive (2003/88/EC), and, for drivers, the EU rules on driving times and rest periods (Regulation (EC) No 561/2006) and the associated national legislation.

The Czech Labour Code sets the maximum standard weekly working time at 40 hours and allows employers to set a shorter weekly working time without a reduction in wages. This provides a clear legal basis for the 100-80-100 model. It is important to note, however, that the specific implementation must be reflected appropriately in employment documentation and internal regulations.

### 12.2 Employment contracts and internal regulations

The project team, with the support of external legal advisers, will review the following documents and update them as required:

- The Company's internal working time regulation (pracovní řád) and shift scheduling rules
- Standard employment contracts and job descriptions
- Remuneration rules, including the treatment of premiums and allowances
- Rules for annual leave, public holidays and other paid leave under the four-day model
- The internal regulation on business travel and on-call duty

For the pilot, the change will be implemented as a temporary measure for the duration of the pilot, with a clear end date and the possibility of extension or reversal depending on the results.

### 12.3 Annual leave and public holidays

Under a four-day model, annual leave entitlements and the treatment of public holidays need to be clearly defined to ensure fairness between employees with different days off. The project team recommends that annual leave continues to be expressed in hours, and that a clear rule is introduced for public holidays falling on an employee's scheduled day off. Detailed rules will be developed during Phase 1.

### 12.4 Part-time, fixed-term and agency workers

Northwind currently employs a small number of part-time employees and uses temporary agency workers during peak periods. Part-time employees will be offered a pro-rata reduction in working time without a reduction in pay, in line with the principle of equal treatment. Agency workers are not directly in scope of the initiative; however, the project team will work with agency partners to ensure that equal treatment obligations are respected.

### 12.5 Employee representation

Northwind does not currently have a trade union or a works council. Nevertheless, the project team recommends that employees are informed and consulted throughout the process, in line with good practice and the spirit of the Labour Code. The employee sounding board described in Section 10.5 will play an important role in this respect.

### 12.6 HR processes and systems

The four-day week will require changes to a number of HR processes and systems, including time recording, payroll, absence management, performance management and recruitment. In particular, the Company's time recording and payroll systems will need to be configured to support the staggered model. These changes are included in the implementation plan and in the one-off cost estimate presented in Section 8.

## 15 Conclusion & Recommendation

### 15.1 Conclusion

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, Northwind Logistics faces a clear choice. It can continue with a traditional working model that is showing increasing signs of strain — rising overtime, increasing absenteeism, high turnover in operational roles and gradually declining service levels — or it can take a bold, forward-looking step that positions the Company as a pioneer in its sector and region.

The analysis presented in this document demonstrates that the four-day week is not only desirable, but also achievable and financially attractive for Northwind:

- **The evidence is strong.** International pilots in the United Kingdom, Iceland and elsewhere show that reduced working time can be introduced without loss of output. Most importantly, the Henley Institute for Workplace Futures' study of 340 European logistics firms demonstrates a **27% increase in productivity** among logistics companies adopting a four-day week — more than enough to offset the 20% reduction in working time.
- **The people case is compelling.** Research consistently demonstrates that burnout drops by half, and studies show that employees on a four-day week are 40% less likely to quit. 81% of Northwind employees would welcome the change.
- **The financial case is robust.** The four-day week is expected to deliver **net annual savings of approximately EUR 1.2 million**, with the one-off implementation investment recovered well within the first year.
- **Customers will be protected.** The staggered-team model ensures that five-day customer coverage is maintained, and experts agree that customer satisfaction is unaffected by a well-planned four-day week.
- **The implementation plan is clear.** A structured, pilot-first approach, supported by strong governance, change management and a robust measurement framework, will ensure a smooth and successful transition.

### 15.2 Recommendation

The Strategy & Transformation Office therefore recommends that the Executive Board:

1. **Approve the introduction of a four-day work week** for all Northwind employees, based on the 100-80-100 model with staggered teams (Option C).
2. **Approve a six-month pilot** from 1 February to 31 July 2027 in warehouse operations (morning shift), dispatch and transport planning, customer service, finance and administration, and IT.
3. **Approve the company-wide rollout** from September 2027, subject to the pilot meeting the success criteria defined in Section 10.4.
4. **Approve the one-off implementation budget** of EUR 240,000 and the recruitment of four additional drivers and two additional warehouse operatives ahead of the company-wide rollout.
5. **Appoint the Managing Director as executive sponsor** and establish the steering committee and project team described in Section 10.5.

### 15.3 Closing remarks

The four-day week represents a unique opportunity for Northwind to invest in its most important asset — its people — while at the same time improving productivity, service quality and financial performance. It is an opportunity to build a more resilient, engaged and future-ready organisation, and to demonstrate that a mid-size logistics company can lead the way in shaping the future of work.

The project team is convinced that, with a holistic approach, strong leadership and the active involvement of every employee, Northwind can make the four-day week a lasting success. We look forward to the Board's decision and to beginning this exciting journey together.

## Appendix A — Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **100-80-100 model** | A four-day week model in which employees receive 100% of their pay for 80% of their working time, in return for a commitment to maintain 100% of their output |
| **Absence rate** | Unplanned absence (mainly sickness) as a percentage of scheduled working time |
| **Compressed hours** | A working pattern in which the full weekly hours are worked over fewer days (e.g. 4 × 10 hours) |
| **Cross-docking** | A logistics practice in which incoming goods are transferred directly to outbound transport with minimal or no storage |
| **Cross-training** | Training employees to perform more than one role, to increase flexibility and coverage |
| **FTE** | Full-time equivalent |
| **HGV** | Heavy goods vehicle |
| **HRIS** | Human resources information system |
| **KPI** | Key performance indicator |
| **Nine-day fortnight** | A working pattern in which employees work nine days in every two-week period |
| **Payback period** | The time required for cumulative net benefits to equal the one-off investment |
| **Slotting** | The placement of products within a warehouse to optimise picking efficiency |
| **SLA** | Service level agreement — a contractual commitment to specific service standards |
| **Staggered teams** | A model in which employees' days off are distributed across the week so that the organisation continues to operate on every working day |
| **TMS** | Transport management system |
| **Voluntary turnover** | The number of employees leaving the organisation of their own accord, as a percentage of average headcount |
| **WMS** | Warehouse management system |
